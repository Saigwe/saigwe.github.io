Semiconductor Yield & Test Analytics

A governed lakehouse for semiconductor manufacturing test data (UCI SECOM, 1,567 wafer runs × 590 sensors). It goes from raw sensor readings to one trusted yield metric, a ranked list of suspect sensors, multivariate anomaly scores, and plain-English questions answered by guardrailed, benchmarked natural-language-to-SQL.

  • Bronze → silver → gold with an explicit grain at every layer: Kimball dimensions, wafer and sensor facts, yield defined exactly once
  • Business-rule tests catch outputs that are "technically valid but semantically wrong": yield ∈ [0,1], fails ≤ wafers, every wafer has readings
  • The same dbt models run on DuckDB (local and CI) and Databricks + Delta Lake (cloud)
  • The NL-to-SQL guardrail allows only single SELECTs against approved gold models; execution accuracy is scored against gold SQL

Revenue-to-GL Reconciliation

A public rebuild, on synthetic data, of the accounting analytics I did for a food-delivery marketplace. An incremental dbt pipeline ties a multi-currency commission sub-ledger (91k events) to the general ledger with $0 unexplained variance on all 26 account-months, explains every difference as a reconciling item, and proposes reclassification journals for revenue booked to the wrong account.

  • An answer-key test plants a duplicate journal, a wrong-FX posting, a late month-end posting and an account-mapping bug. The build fails unless every one is found with the right reason and amount, and nothing else is flagged.
  • The revenue fact is incremental on load time, not event time. A script proves the incremental result equals a full refresh, including events that arrive late.
  • FX is translated at batch level, as the GL does it, with a 5-day fallback for weekends and holidays; dbt unit tests cover the fallback and the variance rules.
  • Dagster assets with a daily schedule and a month-end re-tie job; the same SQL targets DuckDB (local and CI) and Snowflake.

Featured · Analytics engineering + fintech

Payments Fraud & Continuous-Audit Mart

Six continuous-audit controls built as dbt models and measured on 6.36M mobile-money transactions (PaySim, 8,213 fraud). Two controls kept for production catch 99.4% of fraud at 89.8% precision. The simple over-200k transfer rule would raise 409k alerts at 0.7% precision. On a later, unseen time window, a gradient-boosted model only matches the two rules, so the explainable rules stay.

  • Every control is scored for precision and recall against the fraud label. Each gets a production, review or retired status from those results, with the reason recorded. A balance-integrity check that raised 1.18M alerts for 20 frauds was retired from the fraud queue.
  • An exceptions queue with severity, evidence and review status, plus a dbt snapshot that keeps the SCD2 history of every review decision as an audit trail.
  • Answer-key tests on planted mule chains, duplicate payments and fan-in accounts; unit-tested control logic; an enforced contract on the performance report. The full build runs in about 10 seconds on DuckDB.
  • Benford's-law conformity by transaction type, graded with Nigrini's thresholds, plus a frank note on what simulated data can and can't prove.

Featured · Analytics engineering + controls

Merchant Risk-Rating Controls

A public rebuild, on synthetic data, of the risk-rating control app I built to replace a spreadsheet upload for ~20k merchant accounts. Ratings for 20,000 merchants come from scoring rules held as data, and overrides go through a maker-checker approval app with required reasons and period locks. Every action lands in an append-only audit log that dbt re-audits independently: 0 control violations across 2,994 events.

  • Eight scoring rules live in a dbt seed. Signals are unpivoted and joined to the rules, so thresholds and weights change without SQL, and every rating lists the rules behind it.
  • Controls are enforced twice: in the app (20 pytest tests: self-approval, roles, reasons, locks, duplicates) and again in SQL over the whole log. An answer-key test plants five kinds of breach and requires each to be caught.
  • A reconciliation guard on "newly high-risk" counts: a silently failed prior-month join would overstate them 1.9–2.4× on this data, and the build now fails if that happens.
  • Type-2 rating history (gaps and islands) and a dbt snapshot. I verified the full flow in the running app: propose, refused self-approval, checker approval, rating changed.

Featured · Data engineering + IoT reliability

Device Fleet Reliability

The fleet-reliability approach I led for ~680k devices, applied to real data: 30.6 million drive-days from Backblaze's 351,000-drive fleet (Q1 2026). It produces annualised failure rates with confidence intervals, failures per 1,000 units, the age "bathtub" curve, left-truncated survival curves, and a next-quarter replacement forecast (914 expected failures, against 1,027 observed this quarter).

  • The 30.6M rows load in 23 seconds with DuckDB. dbt builds a drive-day fact and AFR by model, age and manufacturer: 1.24% fleet AFR, with 35 models meeting Backblaze's reporting threshold.
  • Conformed keys: 476 drives report a WDC model without its prefix, which would split one model in two. Silver fixes it, keeps the raw value, and a test guards it.
  • Governance built in: owners and classifications as dbt meta, enforced contracts on the AFR reports, a source profile, lineage, and source-quality warnings.
  • SMART 197 and 198 appear on failed drives about 28× more often than on healthy ones. Kaplan–Meier is corrected for drives already in service (left truncation), and the README is open about where single-quarter curves are noisy.

Featured · Data engineering + streaming

Real-Time Device Telemetry Stream

The streaming version of that device reporting: alerts as devices fail, not in the next day's report. 30.7 million real heartbeats (the Backblaze fleet, replayed with deliberate jitter, late arrivals and duplicate deliveries) go through event-time windows with a watermark, deduplication and a late-data side output, at 148k events a second. Every one of 540 windows reconciles to a dbt batch reference, and the batch matches the source's 1,030 failures exactly.

  • Runs end to end through real Kafka (Redpanda) in CI: JSON schema in the registry, a 3-partition topic, offsets committed only after the sink write, then the same dbt reconciliation. Median latency from producer to published window is 0.41 s.
  • The Kafka run found a real bug the ordered replay couldn't: 59% of events went late because the watermark followed the fastest partition. Per-partition watermarks (as in Flink) cut that to 0.3%.
  • Effectively-once results: at-least-once input, deduplication and an idempotent upsert keyed by site and window. An Iceberg table keeps the history of every published window, and 10 pytest tests cover the engine.
  • A mutation test proves the reconciliation catches lost events. A design doc covers batch vs streaming, lateness vs latency, and what changes at 10×.

Featured · Analytics engineering + product

Product Usage, Retention & Experiments

Product analytics on 2.76 million real e-commerce events (RetailRocket), with sessions, funnel, day-N retention and stickiness each defined once in dbt. Alongside it, an A/B testing toolkit validated on 269,683 real visitors: A/A tests, planted effects and a deliberately broken split. A product write-up argues which metric to move and why.

  • Funnel: 2.5% of viewing sessions add to cart, 32.6% of those buy, and 87% of visitors never return. Add-to-cart is the step to move, and its tests need about 3× fewer users than purchase-conversion tests.
  • The toolkit (sample size, sample-ratio check, CUPED, guardrails) recovers planted lifts within 0.3 points with 93–97% interval coverage. CUPED raises power at a +5% lift from 85% to 96% where the pre-period predicts the outcome.
  • Honest findings: CUPED does nothing for conversion (pre-period correlation 0.06), and a 2% guardrail can't be verified at this traffic. A 2% loss of treatment units is caught (p = 2e-10).
  • 27 dbt checks run in 8.5 seconds, including unit tests on the 30-minute session rule and bot exclusion, and a test that every human event lands in exactly one session.
