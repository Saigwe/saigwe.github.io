Semiconductor Yield & Test Analytics

A governed lakehouse on 1,567 real wafer test runs (UCI SECOM, 590 sensors, 93.4% yield). It defines yield once, runs a daily p-chart, ranks the sensors that separate failing wafers beyond chance, ranks wafers for inspection on later, unseen data, and answers plain-English questions through a Claude agent that can't leave the gold layer.   • Control limits come only from earlier, passing wafers, and a dbt unit test proves it. In the later half, passing wafers trip more than twice as many limits (10.9 vs 4.4 per wafer): the process drifted.

  • The NL-to-SQL guardrail uses DuckDB's own parser: 28 of 28 adversarial cases, where the regex it replaced let 6 attacks through. 82/82 dbt checks and 33 pytest tests pass.

Featured · Data engineering + streaming

Real-Time Device Telemetry Stream

The streaming version of that device reporting: alerts as devices fail, not in the next day's report. 30.7 million real heartbeats (the Backblaze fleet, replayed with deliberate jitter, late arrivals and duplicate deliveries) go through event-time windows with a watermark, deduplication and a late-data side output, at 148k events a second. Every one of 540 windows reconciles to a dbt batch reference, and the batch matches the source's 1,030 failures exactly.

  • Runs end to end through real Kafka (Redpanda) in CI: JSON schema in the registry, a 3-partition topic, offsets committed only after the sink write, then the same dbt reconciliation. Median latency from producer to published window is 0.41 s.
  • The Kafka run found a real bug the ordered replay couldn't: 59% of events went late because the watermark followed the fastest partition. Per-partition watermarks (as in Flink) cut that to 0.3%.
  • Effectively-once results: at-least-once input, deduplication and an idempotent upsert keyed by site and window. An Iceberg table keeps the history of every published window, and 10 pytest tests cover the engine.
  • A mutation test proves the reconciliation catches lost events. A design doc covers batch vs streaming, lateness vs latency, and what changes at 10×.

Day-Ahead Load Forecasting & Ops Reports

Five years of half-hourly GB national demand from NESO, backfilled once and then loaded incrementally, with Open-Meteo weather (as forecast the day before) and bank holidays. A LightGBM day-ahead forecast is tested walk-forward against the naive baseline and against NESO's own published forecast. A daily job on GitHub Actions forecasts tomorrow and writes a morning report.

  • Over 45,031 half-hours from April 2024 to October 2026, starting from NESO's forecast and learning its systematic error beats NESO: 2.69% vs 2.78% MAPE, best in 23 of 31 months and every year. Same-day-last-week scores 8.14%.

  • The daily job loads, builds, forecasts, scores and reports in under a minute. It posts to Slack when a webhook is set and keeps its forecast log on its own branch, so accuracy is tracked over time.

IT General Controls & Access-Risk Analytics

Seven access controls (leavers, workstation access, privileged out-of-hours access, removable media, joiners/movers/leavers) tested monthly for 1,000 employees over 17 months of the CERT r4.2 insider-threat benchmark (855k logons, 405k device events). Each control has a KRI with RAG thresholds, and they feed a review-list ranking that is scored against the benchmark's answer key of 70 planted insiders.

  • The ranking's top 10 are all insiders, and its top 50 hold 36 of the 70 (chance would give 3.5). Every insider in the data-theft-before-leaving scenario is in the top 50. No weight was fitted to the answer key, and all three score designs I tried are reported.
  • The test shows which controls detect and which only make noise. First removable-media use flags 35 people, all 35 insiders. Admin out-of-hours access fires for 100% of administrators every month: a badly designed control, not a failing one.
  • HR snapshots describe the month's end: 154 of 155 leavers log on after leaving the directory, so the leaver control measures against the month end. One scenario is out of reach without file and web logs, and the README says so.
  • SCD2 employee history with a 'Left' version, reconciliation tests on every logon and connect, and no names or e-mails in gold, all enforced in the build.

Featured · Data governance + discovery

Data Discovery & Governance Across PostgreSQL, MongoDB and JSON

A discovery tool that profiles a three-system estate (Pagila in PostgreSQL 18, an app team's MongoDB, a partner's JSON feed). It infers keys and links between systems that declare none, classifies personal data, catches drift and numbers sent as text, and scores data confidence. It writes the data dictionary, source map, PII register and dbt sources with generated tests, then is graded against what is known to be true.

  • Against Postgres's declared keys: primary keys 13 of 13, foreign keys found with precision and recall of 0.955 (21 of 22). Across systems, all 8 planted links are found, plus a real natural-key lookup nobody planted.
  • The first run scored 0.50 precision. Four fixes, each with a unit test, got it to 0.955: own-key parents, exact homes beating 1:1 extensions, partition keys mapped to their table, and "address" no longer singularised to "addres".
  • 21 PII fields with no false positives. Fields are judged by path in context, so name.first and cast[].name are people but a category's name is not, and a date is never taken for a phone number.
  • 17 of 19 datasets are ready for governed reporting. The confidence score is explainable line by line, and drift and text-typed amounts pull the app and partner data down.
