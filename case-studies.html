Case study 1 · Accounting analytics · DoorDash (independent consultant, 2026)

Commission revenue that ties to the general ledger with $0 variance

Situation
Commission revenue under ASC 606 depends on a standalone selling price (SSP) analysis across merchants and currencies. It was recalculated by a legacy script, and the external auditors needed the monthly USD results to agree with the general ledger.
Task
Replace the script with a pipeline the accounting team and auditors could rely on: same answer every month, every difference explained.
Action
  • Designed an end-to-end incremental dbt pipeline, from staging through to the reconciliation.
  • Built a reusable FX-rate dimension, so every currency converts the same way in every model.
  • Added merchant floor, median and ceiling range checks, so an implausible SSP is caught before it reaches the numbers.
  • Wrote 25 data tests, and reconciled the monthly USD results to the GL.
Result
$0 variance to the general ledger for the external auditors, and the legacy script-based recalculation retired. In the same accounting work I corrected about $44M of business-line misclassification.
source systems ─▶ staging ─▶ FX-rate dimension ─▶ SSP and commission models
                                                         │
                         merchant range checks + 25 tests ┤
                                                         ▼
                              monthly USD results ─▶ reconciliation to GL ─▶ auditors

What I'd tell a hiring manager: a reconciliation is a product, not a one-off check. It earns trust when it runs every month, explains every difference, and fails loudly before anyone else finds a problem.

Case study 2 · Data discovery and reporting · VoCoVo (independent consultant, 2026)

Making an undocumented device estate reportable

Situation
Device and operational data sat across PostgreSQL, MongoDB and semi-structured JSON, with no data dictionary, no map of how sources related, and recurring reports that took about a day to produce.
Task
As data and reporting lead: find out what the data was and how far it could be trusted, then give the business governed reporting it could plan from.
Action
  • Profiled 56 tables across PostgreSQL, MongoDB and JSON, and prioritised 36 for a governed reporting foundation.
  • Built the data dictionary, a source map, and a model with explicit data-confidence measures, so every figure carries how much it can be relied on.
  • Built estate-wide dashboards and SQL extracts over ~680k devices, which surfaced about 6.4k controllers missing from the earlier reporting scope.
  • Normalised hardware failure rates per 1,000 active units, so products with very different install bases could be compared for end-of-life planning.
Result
Selected recurring reporting cut from about a day to about an hour, on a documented, prioritised data foundation.
PostgreSQL ─┐
MongoDB    ─┼─▶ profile 56 tables ─▶ prioritise 36 ─▶ dictionary + source map + confidence
JSON       ─┘                                               │
                                                            ▼
                              governed reporting model ─▶ estate dashboards (~680k devices)

What I'd tell a hiring manager: before building anything, find out what the data is and how much it can be trusted, and write that down where everyone can see it. The dashboards only became quick once that was done.
